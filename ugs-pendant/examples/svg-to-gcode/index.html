1 · Artwork

Custom coordinates are in the scaled, rotated SVG coordinate system (Y upward). This changes output coordinates, not machine zero.

2 · Tool and passes

Laser passes repeat at focus Z. Match M3/M4 and the S range to your controller. Power is disabled for travel and lowering.

Use Surface Z, Final Z and Step down above for progressive passes, just as in plotter mode. Example: 0 → −1.2 with step 0.5 cuts at −0.5, −1.0, −1.2. On a floating Z axis these Z changes set pressure, not measured material depth.

Add a swivel arc when a corner changes direction by more than this angle. Smaller turns use a straight connection. 0 adds arcs at every nonzero corner; smooth curves do not get per-sample swivels. Entry alignment is separate.

Uncheck to turn corners at the current cutting Z. XY swivel arcs remain. Entry alignment is controlled separately below.

Swivel Z must be at or above the first cutting Z and still keep blade contact. Safe travel Z must fully release and clear the blade.

Leave repeats at 1 for one cut at each Z. All swivel arcs use swivel feed. Only arcs at or above the lift angle raise to swivel Z, when lift is enabled. The lift angle does not decide whether an arc is added.

Uncheck to remove all entry turns and lead-ins. Corner swivels stay enabled. With alignment on, lead-in 0 removes only the straight lead-in; the entry turn remains when direction changes.

Orange lines show swivel arcs and tight continuous holder turns. Continuous turns stay at working feed and cutting Z; the lift angle applies to inserted corner arcs. These lines show the HOLDER center moving, not the blade-tip cut. In an ideal swivel, the holder turns around the tip to face the next cut. Contact turns can still mark material. Positive lead-ins move the tip too; their position is not checked for scrap or neighboring shapes. Knife output uses M5 to turn the spindle off and never turns it on.

3 · Paths

Click near the selected path to lock a start. Open paths use an endpoint. Manual starts and direction choices survive order changes; reimport clears them. Reversing a closed path keeps its start. For open paths, picking an endpoint sets direction; changing direction clears that endpoint lock.

Curve quality and output

Physical SVG dimensions take priority. DPI affects pixel-based sizes. Convert text and clones to paths before importing.

Higher values sample each SVG curve more finely. Extra samples are added to meet the arc step and curve tolerance. Straight lines keep only their endpoints.

Maximum sampling distance along curves and circles. Smaller values mean finer sampling and more data. Also limits knife swivel steps. Output uses straight G1 moves.

Controls curve sampling and short-move cleanup before scaling. Smaller values preserve more detail.

Combines consecutive vertices on one contour before short-move cleanup and knife compensation. Each whole group must fit within this distance; groups cannot grow through a chain of tiny steps. Keeps the incoming and outgoing directions so genuine corners remain. Open endpoints and separate contours stay separate. Start at 0.001 mm; 0.01 mm removes larger tiny details. Distances are before scaling.

Merges nearby samples only when the replacement stays within half the curve tolerance. Endpoints are retained; not every short move can be removed safely.

Closes a single path when its start and end are this close. Does not join separate paths. Use 0 to preserve intentional tiny openings.

Reimport applies the controls above and clears manual starts and directions. Original SVG files are unchanged. Import distances are measured before artwork scaling.

Removes redundant output points within this distance of the sampled path, after scaling. In knife mode this applies to the holder path; it is not a guaranteed blade-tip error. Starts, ends, Z changes and explicit swivels are retained. All tool modes omit repeated feed words.
